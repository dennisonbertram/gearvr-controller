Gear VR Controller, revived

Your old Gear VR controller is a Mac remote now.

Samsung stopped making the $40 Gear VR controller years ago. GearVR Remote is a free menu-bar app that turns it into a gyro air-mouse, a thumb trackpad and a media remote for macOS. Point to move the cursor, pull the trigger to click.

    What the app sends

      Keyboard: click the demo first, then ←↑→↓ aim, Space trigger, H Home, B Back, + − volume.

      What it does

      Everything in the demo is in the app, plus the settings to tune it for your hand.

      Gyro air-mouse with adaptive smoothing

      Point and turn to move the cursor. Turning is measured around the real vertical, so it works however you roll your wrist. Smoothing adapts to speed: slow, careful moves are steadied and fast flicks come through without lag.

      Magnetic buttons

      Buttons, links, tabs, menu items and Dock icons get slightly sticky, so arm wobble can't knock you off them. Past the middle of the slider it becomes a real magnet: the pointer snaps on, a small push hops to the next button, a push into empty space pulls free.

      Re-home clutch

      Arm drifted somewhere awkward? Hold the trigger, touch the bottom of the pad (tink), move your hand back, release (pop). The cursor never moved and nothing was clicked. Lost the pointer entirely? Double-click the trigger and hold the second click: it jumps back to the middle of the screen.

      Four touchpad modes

      Scroll while the air-mouse is on, Cursor like a laptop trackpad, Swipes that send arrow keys or anything you map, and Volume for smooth system volume under your thumb.

      Hold to repeat, touchpad volume

      Vol ± send the same media keys as your keyboard, with the usual macOS volume display, and keep going while held. Volume mode sets the output device directly in fine steps instead of sixteenths.

      Every button remappable

      Trigger, Home, Back, pad click, Vol ± and all four swipes can be a click, a key combo like cmd+[, a media key (play, next, mute, brightness), or a shell command.

      Training mode

      A one-minute aiming exercise: click 15 targets that shrink to the size of a close button. It measures your tremor and accuracy, then suggests speed, smoothing and magnet strength you can apply with one click.

      Recalibrate in two seconds

      If the pointer creeps while your hand is still, put the controller down and press Start. It re-measures the gyro's zero point, and keeps the old calibration if you bumped it.

      Battery care, built in

      The controller runs on two AAA batteries and stays awake as long as an app streams from it. So GearVR Remote lets it sleep: and only asks for the fast sensor stream when it needs it.

      • Sleeps after idle minutes. Five by default, set in Settings → Pointer. The sensors go off, the app lets go, and the controller falls asleep a couple of minutes later.
      • Sleeps with your Mac. The sensors switch off the moment your Mac sleeps or locks.
      • Pause keeps the link. Turning off "Control this Mac" switches the sensors off but stays connected.
      • Press Home to wake. Or click Wake in the menu. The app reconnects on its own. A slow red flash on the controller means new batteries.

      Install

      About two minutes. You need a Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later and a Gear VR controller with batteries in it.

      1. Download the DMG

        Get the latest release . It's a universal app for Apple silicon and Intel.

      2. Drag it to Applications

        Open the DMG and drag GearVR Remote onto the Applications folder.

      3. Open it once, then click Open Anyway

        The app isn't notarized, so macOS says it can't verify the developer. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.

      4. Allow Bluetooth

        Click Allow when macOS asks. The Welcome window shows a checklist that ticks off as you go.

      5. Allow Accessibility

        System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, turn on GearVR Remote. Without it, macOS silently ignores the pointer and clicks.

      6. Press Home on the controller

        The menu-bar glyph fills in when it connects. Set the controller down for a second so the gyro can calibrate, then pick it up and point.

      Don't hold Home

      Holding Home for several seconds puts the controller in pairing mode and wipes its bond with your Mac. If that happens, choose Forget This Device in Bluetooth settings and connect again.

      Or build from source

      The Command Line Tools are enough, no Xcode needed. This builds a universal binary and copies it to /Applications.

      git clone https://github.com/dennisonbertram/gearvr-controller
cd gearvr-controller/mac
./build.sh install

      For hackers

      The controller's Bluetooth LE protocol is documented end to end, verified on real hardware with recorded packets and tests.

      timestampsaccelerometergyroscopemagnetometertouchpadtemperaturebuttonsbattery

      Protocol highlights

      • Write 0800, wait about 1.5 s for the controller to echo it, then 0100: a steady 68 packets/s with no drops. Send them back to back and the stream stops.
      • Each packet carries three IMU samples, with calibrated axes and scales.
      • 0600 / 0700 switch a low-power mode that turns sensors off but stays connected.
      • 0500 returns the real serial number and the touchpad's 320-unit range.
      • A hidden HID consumer-control channel behind a non-standard service UUID.

      Questions

      Which controller do I need?

      The Samsung Gear VR Controller, model ET-YO324 (also listed as SM-R324). It's the one with a round touchpad, Home and Back buttons, a volume rocker and a trigger underneath. It was tested with firmware YO324XXU0AQC1.

      Where can I get one?

      Samsung no longer sells it, but used and new-old-stock units turn up on eBay, local marketplaces and in thrift-store Gear VR bundles, often for less than the original $40. Search for "Gear VR controller" or the model number. It takes two AAA batteries.

      Which version of macOS?

      macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple silicon or Intel. The app is a universal binary.

      Why isn't it notarized or on the App Store?

      It's a free side project, and notarizing needs a paid Apple developer account. The App Store also doesn't allow apps that drive the pointer through Accessibility like this. The source is all on GitHub, so you can read it or build it yourself. The first launch needs one click on Open Anyway in Privacy & Security.

      Accessibility is on, but the pointer doesn't move.

      macOS ties the permission to the app's code signature, so after an update or a rebuild the switch can look on while the permission is gone. Run this in Terminal, relaunch the app and allow Accessibility again:

      tccutil reset Accessibility com.dennisonbertram.gearvr-remote
      I can't see the menu-bar icon.

      On a Mac with a notch, a full menu bar can hide the icon behind it. The app notices and tells you. Hold ⌘ and drag menu-bar icons to make room, or turn on Always show in the Dock in Settings → General. You can also search for "GearVR" in Spotlight; opening it again brings the settings window back.
